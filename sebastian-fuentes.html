About

I grew up between a soccer pitch and a ballet studio, which is a strange pair of childhoods until you notice they're both obsessed with the same thing: how a body moves under control. A good touch on the ball and a clean arabesque are solving the same problem from opposite directions.

Mechanical engineering turned out to be the third version of that question — how do you design a system, a linkage, a gear train, so that motion happens exactly the way you intend, no more and no less. Code became the fourth: a way to choreograph logic instead of limbs.

I don't treat these as separate hobbies stacked on top of each other. They're the same curiosity, applied to different materials — muscle, metal, and syntax.

What I work on

Football

Soccer

Small-sided games on Sunday mornings, mostly. I think about spacing on a pitch the same way I think about tolerances in a drawing — the margin for error is smaller than it looks.

Ballet

Ballet

I came to it late, through a partner's class I wasn't supposed to enjoy this much. Barre work is the closest thing I've found to debugging — small corrections, repeated until they hold.

Software

Coding

Mostly small tools — scripts that automate something I was about to do by hand, prototypes for ideas I want to see move before I commit to building them for real.

Design

Mechanical engineering

My actual training. I like mechanisms you can explain with a single diagram — linkages, gear trains, anything where the motion is the whole idea.
